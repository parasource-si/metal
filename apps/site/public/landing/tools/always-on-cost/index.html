what does it really cost to keep an ai agent running?

the server is rarely the expensive part. your time is.

by the Metal team · checked 12 november 2026 · how we made this

a spare computer costs a few dollars of power a month, a small server costs whatever your host charges, and both cost you every evening spent on updates and restarts. dusk cloud is a flat $20 or $50 a month and we do the upkeep. put your own numbers in below and see which one wins for you.

your numbers

the starting values are examples. change them to yours; nothing you type leaves your browser.

where your bots livea monthwhat that is
your laptop$0free, but only while it's awake and open
a spare computer at home-electricity, plus your time
a server you rent-the hosting bill, plus your time
dusk cloud plus$20$20 a month, or $200 a year; we look after it

every option also uses your own ai plan, which is billed by your ai provider and isn't counted here.

how it works out the numbers

electricity is cheap. it's almost never the deciding number.

a spare computer idling all month:

watts / 1,000 = kilowatts
x hours on per day
x 30.4 days in an average month
x your price per kwh
= your monthly power bill for it

20 w, 24 h, $0.20: 0.02 x 24 x 30.4 x 0.20 = about $2.92

your time is the big one. updates, a restart after a power cut, a backup you forgot to check. one hour a month at $40 an hour already costs more than the power and most small servers.

a rented server is its bill plus that same time. Metal is free to run on your own server. it needs ubuntu 22.04 or 24.04 and 2 gb of memory or more.

dusk cloud is flat. plus is $20 a month or $200 a year. pro is $50 a month or $500 a year. we keep the machine running and back it up every day (pricing).

left out on purpose:

  • buying the spare computer in the first place
  • your internet bill
  • your ai plan, which every option uses and your ai provider bills

reading the result

if you only use bots at your desk, the free row wins. stay on your laptop. no shame in it.

the other rows only matter when you want things to happen without you. the morning brief at 7, a reply from your phone at midnight, a long job that finishes while you sleep.

then compare with your time switched on. a $5 server that eats an evening a month isn't a $5 server. most people who run their own box underprice their evenings.

want the full picture of each option, not just the money? read where ai agents run when your laptop is closed.

a bot that runs around the clock uses your ai plan around the clock. for heavy use, a bigger plan or an api key is the cheaper route.

mistakes people make

leaving your time at zero

even $20 an hour changes the answer. be honest with yourself.

using the watts on the power brick

that's the maximum. idle draw is much lower: check the spec sheet or a plug-in meter.

forgetting the ai plan

a bot working all night uses your ai plan all night. that bill doesn't change with where the bot lives.

questions

where do the starting numbers come from?

they're examples, not measurements. your real watts are on the computer's spec sheet or a plug-in meter. your real power price is on your bill.

does the calculator send my numbers anywhere?

no. it runs in your browser and sends nothing.
