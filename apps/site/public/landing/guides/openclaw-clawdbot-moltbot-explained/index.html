did openai buy openclaw?

openclaw remains independent; its creator joined openai. peter steinberger's new job and the project's move to a foundation are separate events.

by the Metal team · checked 12 november 2026 · how we made this

clawd, clawdbot and moltbot appear in the same project's history. as of 12 november 2026, the foundation says openclaw remains open and independent.1 the dates below untangle the names and the acquisition headlines.

the names belong to one history

clawd, moltbot and openclaw belong to the same project's story. clawd began in november 2025. after anthropic's legal team requested a rename, moltbot followed. openclaw arrived on 29 january 2026.2 clawdbot also appears in a repository docker advisory.6

match a tutorial's date, release and install instructions to the current docs before copying its commands.

the dates that matter

keep the rename, job move and foundation apart. they answer different questions.

  • november 2025: clawd begins. moltbot follows the rename request; the history gives no exact date.2
  • 29 january 2026: the project announces the openclaw name.2
  • 14 february 2026: peter steinberger announces his move to openai. he says openclaw will enter a foundation and stay independent.3
  • 8 july 2026: the foundation launches as a us 501(c)(3) with full-time staff. it says openai committed to keeping the project open and independent.1

the creator joined openai; the project got a foundation

february explains the new job; july explains who looks after openclaw. the creator's post sets out his plans. the foundation announcement describes an independent organisation, rather than an acquisition or an openai product.13

the connection is real: the creator joined openai, and the foundation describes openai's commitment. ownership, support and safety guarantees don't follow from that connection.

what you're choosing today

install the project as it exists today. as of 12 november 2026, openclaw describes an open-source assistant for your computer or server, reached through chat apps. its repository uses mit.45

look at the current setup and upkeep before installing. the alternatives guide compares the work involved. the safety guide separates published advisories, malicious skills and later security work.

follow the headline back to its announcement

the original announcement tells you what actually changed. a creator's post explains their plans. a foundation post describes the organisation. the repository shows the license and code history.

for an acquisition claim, ask for an acquisition announcement. another retelling of the creator joining openai won't do.

for a clawd or moltbot setup guide, match its release to the current project site. old downloads, hosts and lookalike names still need that scrutiny.
