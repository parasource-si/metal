Privacy policy and controller

Effective date: 12 November 2026. Last updated: 16 November 2026.

dusk bot, the operator of the services (“dusk bot”, “we” or “us”), is the controller for personal data processed through the dusk bot services described here. You can contact privacy@dusk.bot about your information or this policy. Our website is dusk.bot. Our optional account dashboard is dusk.bot/dashboard. Our link service is link.dusk.bot.

Your computer, VPS, our account services and a dusk cloud desktop have different roles. Choosing a hosted desktop stores your bot content on that machine. That content is not part of account dashboard workspace reports.

Desktop app and AI providers

dusk bot is local-first. On macOS, Windows and Linux, your bots, chats, files, memory and settings live on your computer or your own server. The app sends anonymous usage info to our account service, including short descriptions your own AI writes on your device about how a session went, with no personal content, described under Product analytics. It is on by default and you can switch it off under Settings, Privacy, "help improve dusk bot". Our account and link services do not receive your message content, prompts, files or AI keys as workspace reporting data.

You sign in to AI engines yourself or add your own API keys. Examples include Anthropic Claude, OpenAI Codex/ChatGPT, Google Gemini and xAI Grok. Prompts and replies go directly from the machine running your bots to the provider under your account. dusk bot does not receive them through the account service. The provider's own terms and privacy policy apply. Read those policies before you send personal or confidential information.

Phone app and push notifications

The phone app lets you use dusk bot with the machine running your bots. Local-first content stays on your own computer or VPS, or on your dusk cloud desktop if you choose one. Phone and device names can appear in the periodic workspace reports described below.

The phone push relay sees your device push token, the bot's name and identifiers. It never sees message text and keeps nothing. It uses Apple Push Notification service and Firebase Cloud Messaging from Google to deliver notifications. Those providers receive the necessary push delivery data.

Crash and error reports

Released desktop apps send crash and error reports while "help improve dusk bot" is on in the app, which it is unless you turn it off. A report contains the error type, a scrubbed message, stack frames with app-relative file names, the page path, the app version, the platform and the system version.

Scrubbing removes secrets, email addresses, web addresses, quoted text and personal folder names. Reports never contain message text, prompts, files, contacts or keys. Reports go to our own account service and are stored in our own database on our server. We do not use a third-party error tracking service. Error reports are on by default and follow the one "help improve dusk bot" switch under Settings, Privacy, in the desktop app; turning it off stops crash reports from the next app start. Similar reports are grouped into one record, which is deleted 30 days after the error was last seen. This reporting is for finding and fixing errors, not product analytics.

Optional account service

If you use the account dashboard at dusk.bot/dashboard, we store your email address and optional display name. For linked Google or Apple sign-in, we store the provider name, its account identifier and email address. Sign-in codes are emailed and kept in memory only.

Sign-in session records contain your device name, platform, app version, creation time and last-seen time. We store only a hash of the session token. The hash lets the service check a session without storing the original token in its session records.

If you link a workspace, we store its name and kind. Periodic workspace reports contain counts and states only. They can include CPU, memory and disk statistics; bot names, colours and models; bot computer states; phone and device names; and daily usage totals. These reports never contain messages, prompts, files or secrets. Names and identifiers may still be personal data even when message content is absent.

Product analytics

We keep first-party usage records in our own database, never with a third-party analytics provider, to see whether people find the site, sign up, set up dusk cloud and get their first bot reply.

  • On the website (dusk.bot and the preview site), a small script of ours records page views (the path only, with ids removed), clicks on sign-up and download links, clicks on other labelled buttons and links (for a link to another site, only its host name), waitlist sign-ups (never the address), whether a sign-up form was started or left unfinished, how far a page was scrolled, how long the page and each of its sections were visible, repeated rapid clicks, media plays, a count of page errors, and the window width. Where cookies are allowed it sets two first-party cookies: dusk_vid, a random visitor id kept for a year, and dusk_sid, a visit id that ends after 30 minutes without activity. If Cloudflare places your connection in the EU, the EEA, the UK or Switzerland (including their territories with their own country code), or cannot tell where you are, the script stores nothing in your browser: no cookies and no local storage (other than the ?dusk_ignore=1 off switch, if you set it yourself), and it removes dusk_vid and dusk_sid if an earlier visit left them. Instead our server derives a visitor id from your IP address and browser type with a secret that is kept only in memory and replaced every day, so the id changes daily and cannot be traced back to your address or linked from one day to the next; we never store the address or the secret. Your visit then ends after 30 minutes without activity, and each visit's source (referring site or campaign) is counted on its own rather than being tied to earlier visits. Until our server has answered, the script stores nothing anywhere. We keep the referring site's name (never its full address), campaign tags (utm_*, ref; values that look like email addresses are dropped), a coarse device, system and browser type, and the country, region and city Cloudflare derives from the connection, with coordinates rounded to about 10 km. We never store IP addresses. When you sign in to the account page on the same domain, your visitor id is linked to your account so we can see which visits led to a sign-up. Without cookies, that link covers only the same day's visits from the same connection and browser.
  • In the account page and the apps: sign-ups and sign-ins (method and device type), onboarding steps, plan and checkout steps, dusk cloud states, which screens and features of the desktop and phone apps are used (names like "routine created", never their content), message and reply counts and how long replies took, error and pairing failure codes, app platform and version. The apps use a random install id; once an app signs in, its install is linked to your account. We never record message content, prompts, files, names you give things, form input, emails or keys.
  • From your bots' work (desktop, phone and dusk cloud): when your workspace is linked to your account, it reports one structured record per bot turn and per routine run. A record holds only words from fixed lists, counts and times: the AI engine family (like claude or codex, never a model name you typed), whether the turn worked, failed or was stopped, a failure reason code (like rate limit or sign-in expired, never the error text), how long it took, the kinds of tools used (like browser, files or terminal), which connected app was used (only by its catalog name, like gmail or notion), a coarse task category worked out on your device from those tools and apps (like email, calendar, research or coding), whether a person, a routine or another bot started it, whether it was in a room, and whether it ran on a desktop or a cloud. A turn also records how long the first reply took, the public model family when it is a known one (like a Claude Sonnet or GPT model; a model name you typed for your own endpoint is recorded only as "other"), which tool kinds and apps had an error, the catalog names of the connected-app actions used (like "send email" in Gmail), and two short codes that stand for the bot and the chat: they are made on your device from a random secret that never leaves it, so we can count turns per bot and per chat without learning which bot or chat it was. Routine runs add how they were started (schedule, app event or webhook), how long they took, how often the routine is set to run (like daily or hourly) and the same kind of code for the routine, so we can see whether routines keep running. The workspace also reports how many bots, rooms, routines, webhooks, skills, chats and archived chats it has, how many bots use each AI engine, and the size of the bots' memory as a range (like "under 100 KB"). It also reports a few named events, each with fixed fields only: an AI sign-in started, finished or failed (with a reason code), a file attached (its type, like image or PDF, and a size range, never its name), which setting changed (the setting's name, never its value), and a chat deleted.
  • How things went (desktop app and dusk cloud, signed in or not): a few events with fixed fields only, so we can see what goes wrong, where people get stuck and what gets done. They are: a turn that failed (engine family and reason code), was stopped by you, or finished using tools (how many tool kinds, and the task category); a tool kind or connected app that had an error; a routine run that finished or failed; an approval card you answered (granted or denied, and what kind of change it was); a change a bot made to its own setup because you asked it in chat (like its model or profile, never the new value); a request the app refused or a policy blocked (a fixed code like "bot limit" and its status); a bot or room created; the type of a file a bot's reply carried (never its name); an AI sign-in, built-in browser download or app update that failed (a reason code); an app process that crashed (which process and the system's reason word); Settings closed without a change, and how long it was open; an @devs report sent; and whether your next message right after a reply asked the same thing again, reworded it, retried after a failure, asked what the bot meant, asked why or how to do something, or said the bot was wrong. That last one is worked out on your device from your message's first words; the message itself is never sent or stored. The desktop app sends these with its random install id even when it is not signed in, and they follow the same "help improve dusk bot" switch.
  • About your setup (desktop and phone apps): once per app start, the operating system and processor type, the app language, a screen size range, light or dark appearance, the chosen skin, whether notifications are allowed, and the hour and weekday on your device. Together with app opens, closes and versions, this shows sessions per day, session length and when people use the app.
  • On the service itself: from our own billing records, how many checkouts are started, paid or left unpaid, plan upgrades and downgrades, and the reason you pick from the list when you cancel. From the existing events, when each feature is first used and how long after sign-up, and how long a sleeping dusk cloud takes to wake. Tool names, arguments, error messages and anything you wrote are turned into those words on your device and never sent.
  • How sessions went: when a chat seems to go badly (signals like a thumbs down, stopping a reply, asking the same thing again, or replies that failed), after a big or long session in one chat (about 15 turns or more, or about 20 minutes of the bot working, once the chat has been quiet for 20 minutes), and once a week for people who use dusk bot regularly, your own AI engine, on its cheapest model and at your plan's cost, writes a few anonymous sentences on your device about how it went: the kind of work, which features and apps were used, what worked and what got in the way (like "connecting gmail took 3 tries"). A filter on your device then removes email addresses, web addresses, handles, file names and folder paths, long numbers and ids, quotes and names, including names the chat itself used and your bots' names. It keeps only app names and small counts, and drops the description if it repeats a run of words from the chat or if too much had to be removed. Our account service checks it again when it arrives. A big session's record also holds its number of turns, its length in minutes, how many turns failed, and the same kinds of words as a turn (engine family, tool kinds, app catalog names, task category). We never receive your messages. How often this happens grows with how much you use the app and is capped each day and each week. These descriptions help us see what to fix first.
  • Your choices: the website script does nothing when your browser sends Global Privacy Control or Do Not Track. Usage info, the descriptions of how sessions went and crash reports share one switch, "help improve dusk bot", which is on by default. You can turn it off in the desktop app under Settings, Privacy, which stops your workspace from sending them, or on your account page under Privacy (PUT /v1/account/analytics), which deletes your recorded events and stops new ones from being kept.
  • Retention: raw events are deleted after 400 days, and raw records of your bots' work and the descriptions of how sessions went after 90 days. Per-day counts linked to you, and the first and last run of each routine code, are deleted after 400 days. Daily totals and chart notes without any personal link are kept. Deleting your account deletes the events, records and descriptions linked to it.
  • Only the operator can see these records, in an admin panel that logs each opening of a person's record.

Waitlist

If you join the waitlist on dusk.bot, we store your email address, your place in line, when you joined, the page or campaign you came from and a short hash of your browser type, in our own database and on a Brevo contact list. We use it only to tell you when dusk bot opens to you. Every email sent to the list has an unsubscribe link, and you can ask privacy@dusk.bot to remove you at any time. Joining the waitlist does not create an account.

Payments and transactional email

Dodo Payments is the merchant of record for paid plans. It handles card data, tax and invoices. dusk bot stores subscription status, plan lines, amounts and payment identifiers. We never store card numbers. Dodo's handling of payment data is also subject to its own applicable privacy terms.

We use Brevo for transactional email, including sign-in codes and account and billing notices. It processes your delivery address and the email content needed to send those messages. Transactional messages are part of providing account and billing functions. They are not product analytics or advertising.

dusk cloud desktops

dusk cloud provides a hosted, always-on computer per customer on InstaBox infrastructure, which runs on Amazon Web Services. InstaBox is a sub-processor. Your bots, chats and files live on that machine. This is the exception to the statement that your message content is not stored on our services. The cloud desktop connects directly to the AI providers you sign in to, using your accounts with them.

Cloud desktop backups are encrypted with a key held by the account service. That key arrangement is different from the link relay, where only your app and your VPS hold the keys. After your plan ends, the desktop is kept for 30 days and then deleted; we email you 7 days before. If you ask to delete it yourself, it is deleted 7 days after you confirm. While the desktop exists we keep its newest three verified backups and one at least a week older. When it is deleted after those 30 days, its encrypted backups are kept 30 more days as a safety copy and then deleted; when you ask for the deletion yourself, the backups are deleted with it.

If you enable Wake for Slack, the account service holds your bot's Slack app-level token while the desktop sleeps. The token is sealed at rest. It also holds Slack events addressed to your bot, sealed to the desktop's key, for at most 24 hours. It never reads or logs event text. You choose whether to enable this feature.

Link service

The service at link.dusk.bot pairs your own VPS with your app. Traffic is relayed as AES-256-GCM frames sealed with keys held only by the app and VPS. The link service cannot read that traffic.

The service stores install session records and hashes of link tokens. These support installation and pairing without storing the original link tokens in those records. An install session ends within two hours and its record is deleted about 15 minutes after it ends. Token hashes for a paired VPS are kept while that pairing exists and are removed with it. Push delivery through this service follows the phone relay description above.

Connected apps

You can optionally connect third-party apps such as Gmail, Slack and GitHub through Composio. Tool traffic passes through a dusk bot broker on Cloudflare Workers to Composio. Composio holds the OAuth connections. The broker stores a hash of each installation's token, its Composio connection identifiers, and incoming app events (such as a new-email notice) until your workspace collects them, for at most 7 days.

Tool traffic can include information needed to carry out your requested action in a connected app. This is distinct from collecting your chats or prompts in account reports. Review the permissions you grant and the connected app's own policy. Composio keeps your connections until you disconnect them, and handles tool data under its own retention rules and privacy policy. The broker token hash is kept while the installation stays connected.

Website, hosting and logs

The website, account service, link service and our database run on a server rented from Contabo GmbH in the European Union. Cloudflare sits in front of dusk.bot as a proxy and content delivery network, runs the connected-apps broker and the cloud desktops' public edge, provides DNS and forwards mail sent to our dusk.bot addresses. Spaceship is the domain registrar. Let's Encrypt provides TLS certificates.

Server access logs include IP addresses. We keep them for 14 days for security and abuse prevention, then they are deleted. Encrypted backups of account service data are made daily and deleted within 30 days: daily copies after 7 days, one weekly copy after 4 weeks. They are encrypted on our server before they leave it, with a key kept off that server, and stored with Oracle Cloud and Cloudflare R2, which cannot read them. We do not use advertising or third-party analytics. Visiting the website does not give us access to content held on your computer or VPS.

Purposes and legal bases

Where GDPR applies, we rely on the following legal bases. Contract covers processing needed to provide the services you request. This includes account sign-in, linked workspace reporting, paid plan administration, cloud hosting, pairing, push delivery and connected-app actions.

Legitimate interests cover keeping services secure, preventing abuse and diagnosing errors. We consider your rights when relying on these interests. You can object to processing based on legitimate interests. Product analytics, including the descriptions of how sessions went, rely on legitimate interests in understanding and improving dusk bot, and you can switch them off at any time as described under Product analytics. Consent applies to the waitlist and to optional sign-in with Google or Apple. You withdraw it by unsubscribing from the waitlist or unlinking the sign-in method on your Account page. Withdrawing consent does not affect earlier lawful processing.

Legal obligations apply to payment records: Dodo Payments keeps invoices and transaction records as merchant of record, and we keep our billing records (plans, amounts and payment identifiers) for as long as tax and accounting law requires. We do not use your data for ads, and our product analytics stay first-party, as described below. We do not collect your message content on our account or link services. Your own dusk cloud desktop and the optional tool traffic described above have separate processing roles.

Sub-processors and service providers

We use a small number of service providers to run dusk bot: Contabo (hosting), Cloudflare (proxy, DNS, connected-apps broker, cloud edge, email forwarding and encrypted backup storage), Oracle Cloud (encrypted backup storage), InstaBox on Amazon Web Services (dusk cloud desktops), Dodo Payments (payments), Brevo (email and the waitlist), Composio (connected apps), Apple and Google (push notifications), Spaceship (domain registrar) and Let's Encrypt (TLS certificates). The subprocessors page lists each one with its purpose, the data involved and where it processes data.

Dodo Payments acts as merchant of record and is an independent controller for the payment data it collects. Google and Apple are independent controllers for the sign-in data they hold about you. The other providers process data on our behalf as sub-processors. A provider's company location does not establish where all processing occurs.

AI model providers you choose have a direct relationship with you. They are not our sub-processors. On dusk cloud, the cloud desktop connects to the providers you sign in to. You still use your own provider account and their terms apply; those connections are part of operating your hosted desktop.

International transfers

Your information may be processed outside your country through these providers. Where a transfer requires safeguards, we use applicable mechanisms, including Standard Contractual Clauses where needed. Our own servers and database are in the European Union. dusk cloud desktops run in the United States (AWS us-west-2), and some providers, such as Cloudflare, Composio, Apple and Google, process data in the United States or on global networks. For those transfers we rely on the provider's Standard Contractual Clauses or an adequacy decision, such as the EU-US Data Privacy Framework, where the provider is certified. Contact privacy@dusk.bot to ask about safeguards applicable to your data or request a copy where available.

Retention and account deletion

Account records, linked workspaces and their latest reports are kept while your account exists and are deleted when you delete it. A session ends when you sign out or when it goes unused: a browser session expires 30 days after sign-in, an app session after 90 days, and an app session in regular use is extended by up to 90 days. Expired sessions can no longer be used. Billing records are kept for as long as tax and accounting law requires, which can be several years after your plan ends, even after the account is deleted. The service-specific periods above apply to analytics, logs, error reports, link records and cloud data.

You can use Delete account on the Account page. It removes the account, sessions, identities and linked workspaces, and ends any work license on it at once. Active plans and cloud desktops must be cancelled first. The page offers to cancel them. A cloud desktop of a deleted account is deleted right away, with its machine and any archived copy; its backups follow the backup rule above. Deleting your dusk bot account does not itself delete local files or records held independently by your AI providers.

Your rights and choices

Depending on applicable law, you can ask to access, correct or delete your personal data, restrict processing or object to it, and obtain a portable copy. On the Account page, Download your data gives you a JSON file. This exports account service data, not an independent copy of every file on your machines.

Email privacy@dusk.bot for correction or other requests. Include enough information to identify your account and request, without sending passwords or keys. We may need to verify your identity before acting. You can complain to your competent supervisory authority. You do not have to contact us before doing so.

Security measures

We use encryption in transit, sealed relay frames, hashed session and link tokens, and encrypted cloud backups. Wake for Slack tokens are sealed at rest, and queued events are sealed to the desktop's key. These measures protect different parts of the service. No system can guarantee complete security. Keep your own devices, provider accounts and keys secure. Report vulnerabilities to security@dusk.bot.

Cookies and local storage

On dusk.bot (the dashboard, the admin and the API), a session cookie (dusk_dash) is HttpOnly and lasts 30 days from sign-in. A CSRF cookie (dusk_csrf) protects requests. A short-lived OAuth state cookie (dusk_oauth, 10 minutes) supports sign-in. Your theme and notification preferences, and interface state such as the workspace you picked and hints you dismissed, are stored in local and session storage. Our cookie policy lists every cookie and the storage items we use.

There are no advertising cookies. Outside the EU, the EEA, the UK and Switzerland, the website's analytics script sets two first-party cookies, dusk_vid (a random visitor id, one year) and dusk_sid (a visit id, 30 minutes), described under Product analytics; they are never shared with anyone. Where Cloudflare places you in those countries, or cannot place you, the script sets no cookies and uses no local storage, and our server counts visits with a daily-changing id instead, as described under Product analytics. The account cookies are strictly necessary for account and security functions. Theme local storage records your preference. Blocking required cookies may prevent account sign-in or related functions from working.

Children, changes and contact

dusk bot is not for children under 16. If you believe a child has provided personal data through our services, contact privacy@dusk.bot so we can address it.

We may update this policy. For material changes, we will email account holders and post a notice on dusk.bot at least 30 days before the change takes effect, unless a shorter period is needed for legal or security reasons. Check the effective date when reviewing it. For privacy requests, contact privacy@dusk.bot. For general questions, use hello@dusk.bot.
