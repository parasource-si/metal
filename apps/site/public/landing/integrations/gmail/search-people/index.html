Metal/integrations/gmail

look up a gmail contact before choosing an address

you remember the photographer, just not where you put their address. give the bot a name, nickname, email, phone clue or company.

by the Metal team · checked 12 november 2026 · how we made this

it searches saved google contacts and, if you ask, people you contacted without saving. get names and possible addresses in chat, then choose the person you recognise. the broader search returns fewer details.

search people, in plain words

get the address without digging through old email. a surname, phone fragment or company gives the bot somewhere to start. the lookup leaves contacts unchanged and writes no email.

saved contacts can return company details. including unsaved contacts limits results to names, email addresses and phone numbers. need the company too? ask for a separate saved-contact search.

a routine to copy

paste this into a chat with your bot

every thursday at 1, read visitor names for the coming week from the visitor-list file in the project folder.

- search my work gmail saved contacts for each name
- include unsaved contacts only when saved contacts have no match
- show names and possible email addresses here
- flag namesakes and say which searches finished

never choose between uncertain matches, edit contacts or message anyone.
when
thursdays at 13:00
needs
gmail connected with read only and contact-reading access; the visitor-list file in the project folder

what comes back looks like this (an example)

visitor addresses
leila patel: one saved contact, leila@brindlewick.example.
second visitor: two possible matches; surname needed to choose.
unsaved-contact search finished for names without a saved match.

good jobs for it

  • recover a booking contact. search for the photographer whose surname you remember.
  • find a repair number. look up the equipment contact you saved.
  • place a visitor before they arrive. match saved contacts to the firm on your visitor list.
  • recover an unsaved address. include people you emailed without adding to contacts.

before it runs

Metal counts Search People as a read: it looks and changes nothing. a bot you allow only to read can use it.

the permissions Gmail asks for to run it: contacts, contacts.readonly, contacts.other.readonly.

how to start

  1. connect the right address book. choose the google account holding your contacts and keep this bot read only.
  2. try someone you know. ask for their name and address, then compare them with your address book.
  3. choose the search scope. say whether it may include unsaved contacts.
  4. give it the visitor file. open automations, confirm thursday at 13:00 and read the first report.

limits worth knowing

  • 30 matches is the ceiling. add a surname or company clue when a common name fills the list.
  • one search can fail while the other succeeds. ask which contact sources finished.
  • unsaved contacts return fewer details. search saved contacts separately for company information.

from composio's catalog for Gmail (action GMAIL_SEARCH_PEOPLE, read 12 november 20264). your bot fills these in from what you tell it.

two people with the same name? give the bot a company or surname instead of letting it pick.

try it in 2 minutes

  1. connect the google account that holds your contacts
  2. ask for a person by surname or company
  3. request names and possible addresses only
  4. compare the address and company with the person you know

mistakes people make

trusting the first namesake

compare the address and company. let the bot show uncertain matches.

asking for company details in the broad search

run a saved-contact search separately; unsaved-contact results omit that detail.

questions

can it find someone i never saved?

yes, ask it to include unsaved contacts. these are people you interacted with, rather than entries you saved.

can i search by company?

yes, search your saved contacts for the company. those results can return its name too. the broader unsaved-contact search leaves company details out.

does looking someone up add them to contacts?

no, the lookup only reads. your address book stays as it was.
