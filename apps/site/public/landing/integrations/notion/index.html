a notion bot that keeps the next step easy to find

find out who owns the next step before the project meeting. connect notion to a Metal bot and ask for incomplete records.

by the Metal team · checked 12 november 2026 · how we made this

it can find pages, read text and update allowed entries. choose the ai you want. start with the gap report here, then decide which page or database fields it may change.

what a bot can do in Notion

get the next step out of the meeting notes and into view. these actions find the project page, read its records and add an update where you want it. name the source and destination separately.

actionMetal treats it aswhat you would use it for
Search Notion pages and databasesreadfind the project home by its title
Get page markdownreadread a document as text before suggesting a revision
Fetch All Notion Block Contentsreadcollect page blocks for a content review
Fetch Databasereadinspect the database before interpreting its records
Fetch database rowreadread the owner, date and next step on one project record
Fetch commentsreadinclude review comments when assessing an unfinished page
Append text blocks (paragraphs, headings, lists)changeadd a dated decision note beneath an agreed heading
Update Database Row (Page)changeupdate a specific record after you define the field rules

composio, the connection service behind Metal's apps, lists 60 Notion actions (version 20261101_00, read 12 november 20265). Metal treats 24 as reads, 2 as sends and 34 as changes.

only actions starting with a read word (get, list, search) count as reads, so a read-only bot can't call the rest. a send reaches people or moves money, like create comment, and waits for your Allow unless you set a daily amount.

connect Notion

  1. open Connected apps in the sidebar.
  2. pick Notion and sign in on Notion's own page, so your password goes to them and never to a bot.
  3. choose which bots may use it, and whether each may only read or may also change things. your bookkeeper doesn't need your Notion.
  4. ask in plain words. reading and drafting happen freely; sending asks you first.

get a report from the page your team already uses. connect notion through connected apps and choose the bot handling documentation. notion handles the sign-in.

paste the source page reference in chat. name any destination separately, and ask what the bot found before enabling upkeep.

routines to copy

keep missing fields, decisions and old instructions as separate jobs. each needs a named source. the bot should report a gap before it tries to fill one.

find incomplete records

each monday at 2, read the linked notion project database. group records missing an owner, next step or target date by project. show the missing field beside each record. never guess or edit. if all are complete, say so in one line.

mondays at 14:00 · a checklist of incomplete project records

prepare decision notes

every thursday at 4, read this week’s entries on the bellwether meeting-notes page in notion. collect only agreed decisions and their stated reasons. compare them with the decision log. draft missing additions here. ask me before you change anything in notion.

thursdays at 16:00 · proposed additions to the decision log

find old instructions

on the first monday each month at 10, read the linked notion onboarding pages and their comments. flag dated instructions, unresolved questions and references an owner needs to revisit. explain why each needs attention. never rewrite or archive pages.

first monday of the month at 10:00 · pages to inspect, with a reason for each

what it asks you first

choose the heading it may add to, then keep the rest out of reach. read only blocks page and database changes. a writer can use an exact action list and a confirmation step in the routine. page edits don't wait for a sending card.

comments count as posts, so sending controls apply. leave comment creation disabled for a private review.

careful with Full access: a bot there with no sending rule can send up to 25 a day without asking. give it Ask every time.

what it cannot do

  • a reader can give you the report privately. read only blocks rewriting, moving and archiving pages.
  • leave missing owners and dates as questions for the team. the bot can use facts in records and notes; it should leave gaps blank.
  • put recurring upkeep on an awake computer. local jobs stop during sleep or when dusk closes. dusk cloud hosts the bot instead.

name the source and destination separately

turn an agreed decision into a note the team can find again. meeting notes hold the evidence; the decision log is the destination. name both and the headings where additions belong.

if delivery work lives in linear, link to that tracker. prose about progress can lag behind the work.

a suggestion in the notes stays a suggestion. only agreed decisions belong in the log.

choose where your notes actually live

notion pages and an obsidian folder need different access. notion uses connected app actions. obsidian uses files on your computer.

choose the source your team maintains, rather than making a second copy no one owns.

for the wider planning week, see the product manager workflow. start with one small upkeep job before asking a bot to maintain everything.

try it in 2 minutes

  1. connect notion and choose read only
  2. paste one project page or database link
  3. ask for missing owners, dates and next steps
  4. open a flagged record and see whether the field is missing

find the unowned next step on monday, before it becomes friday’s missed deadline.

mistakes people make

asking it to fill missing dates

ask for a gap report unless a source states the date.

using one page as both evidence and permission

name where the facts come from and where edits may go.

questions

can i use claude with notion pages?

yes, choose claude or another ai for the bot. connect notion and grant only the actions that page job needs. the bot keeps its own chats and memory.

can it maintain my wiki without changing everything?

begin with a reader’s report. for writing, name the destination, allowed edit and ask-first instruction.

does a bot remember the way we write project notes?

yes, tell it the rules you want kept. read and edit its notes under the bot’s memory settings.
