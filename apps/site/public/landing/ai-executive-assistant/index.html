an ai executive assistant for inbox and calendar work

walk into the call knowing what you promised last time. a Metal bot reads your gmail threads and google calendar, brings you meeting context and drafts replies.

by the Metal team · checked 12 november 2026 · how we made this

it can also find possible times for your next catch-up. start with reading. get the agenda and open questions in your chat, then choose what deserves your time.

give it the accounts it actually needs

give it the inbox and calendar behind your day. make a bot with this job: "help me prepare for meetings and correspondence. use only named accounts. flag uncertainty. ask before changing anything."

connect gmail and google calendar in connected apps. label work and personal accounts clearly; dusk supports up to five usable accounts per app.

start read only. find event and find free slots read your calendar; gmail fetches mail and threads.12 the first three routines need no meeting edits. see gmail and google calendar for setup.

routine one: the day before it starts

find the clash before you're late to it. send:

"every weekday at 8:15am, read today's work calendar. return a time-ordered agenda here. include stated locations, missing details and overlapping meetings. don't change events or guess travel time."

open the overlapping events and decide which matters. the brief has already done the hunt.

read the schedule card. wrong hour? ask which time zone it uses. set yours in about me so every bot gets it.58

routine two: possible times, no invitations

find a catch-up time and leave yourself a lunch break. try:

"every monday at 9:30am, find free slots in my work calendar for this week's catch-ups. look for 30 minutes between 10am and 4pm. return options here. never create, move or invite. these are my free times, not anyone else's confirmed availability."

use the find free slots read action. leave room for preparation or an actual break before offering a time.

need the week's pressure points instead? use the calendar preview. keep the routine that gives you something useful.

routine three: the thread before the meeting

know what you promised before joining the call. send:

"every weekday at 4pm, read tomorrow's work calendar. for contacts named in your job, read related gmail threads. give me open questions and explicit commitments here. say when a thread match is uncertain. change nothing."

name people or threads for the first runs. repeated subjects can belong to different conversations.

open the original thread when a promise matters. the meeting prep routine gives this job more room.

routine four: shorter replies, with a stopping point

turn the thread into a reply you only have to finish. gmail's create email draft needs write access.2 allow the reads and that action alone.

"at noon each weekday, read new direct requests from correspondents named in your job. use their threads and your memory notes for context. propose replies here using facts you can verify. ask me about commitments or sensitive judgments. ask before saving gmail drafts. never send."

the ask-before-drafting line is your instruction; draft edits don't ask automatically. for enforced reading access, keep proposed replies here and save them yourself.

routine five: close the day without missing a question

leave work knowing which questions still need you. try:

"every weekday at 5:15pm, read today's work email threads. list unanswered direct questions and dates promised in the messages. quote only enough to identify each request. return the list here. silence is not a promise. never chase, label or archive."

read the thread's last reply before answering. narrow the senders or pause the routine if it repeats work you've handled.

watch the early runs. add the next routine when the first one helps.

what stays with you

let it carry the context so you can handle the conversation. keep disputes, hiring conversations and important negotiations with a person. the bot can prepare the thread and open questions. you own the relationship.

calendar edits and inbox labels don't automatically wait for approval. keep access read only where possible. for writes, retain "ask me before you change anything".

local work stops during sleep or when dusk closes. runs under 12 hours late run once on return; older runs skip. automations shows schedules and history. pause routines that no longer help.5

try it in 2 minutes

  1. make one bot for meeting and email preparation
  2. connect work gmail and calendar, read only
  3. ask for today's agenda without changes
  4. open one meeting and the email thread behind it

the note earns its keep when you join the call already knowing the awkward question.

before adding another routine

  • the existing one returns something you actually read
  • its account and time zone are right
  • it names uncertain matches instead of guessing
  • you still approve commitments and sensitive replies

mistakes people make

letting an open slot become an invitation

ask only for options. decide whether you want that time booked.

letting the assistant guess which thread matters

name the contact or source thread for early runs.

turning on five routines before checking one

start with the agenda. add another only after the first earns its place.

questions

can an ai assistant clean up my gmail inbox?

start by identifying messages. these routines read threads and prepare replies. archive, delete and label actions are different jobs. keep access read only while you work out the rules.

will it book meetings automatically?

these routines only find events and possible times. you choose and book. calendar edits need an explicit ask-first instruction if you allow writes.

can i connect separate work and personal gmail accounts?

yes, with separate labels. dusk supports up to five usable accounts per app. name the account in each routine.

is an ai executive assistant free?

personal desktop use is free; ai usage stays separate. work use needs a license. cloud pro includes one for one person. your ai provider still bills usage.

free on your own computer, with every feature. no account, no card.
