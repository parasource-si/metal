docs/using bots well

bots and chats

making a bot, picking its ai, and talking to it while it works, by text or by voice.

a bot is a teammate with one job. it has a name, its own chats, its own memory and the ai you picked for it. you text it, it works, it tells you what it did.

make a bot

choose new bot. give it a name, a shape and a colour (that's how you spot it in the sidebar), and tell it its job in plain words. tips for using bots has help with the job.

right-click a bot in the sidebar to pin, rename, archive or delete it; duplicate it from its side panel. deleting a bot deletes its routines too; archive it if you only want it out of the way.

which ai it uses

every bot has its own model. open the bot and use the model picker. give your researcher one ai and your writer another, and swap whenever you like. the bot keeps its chats, files and memory; only the thinking changes.

if your ai plan runs out, the bot says so in the chat. wait for the reset, or switch it to another ai and carry on.

while it works

replies stream in, and a small line shows each tool it uses or page it opens. you can:

  • send another message to steer it, or queue it for when it's done.
  • stop it with ⌘. (Ctrl. on windows and linux).
  • answer its questions. when it needs a yes, a card with allow and Deny appears and it waits.

a bot can keep several threads, so a quick question doesn't bury a long project. memory and job are shared across them.

voice

  • press the speaker on any reply to hear it.
  • press the microphone in the message box to talk instead of typing.
  • choose call to talk a problem through. Space interrupts, Esc hangs up.

give each bot its own voice in its settings, under voice & alerts. the built-in mac voices are free; elevenlabs, fish audio and grok voices use your own key.

handy keys

jump to any bot, or search every chat⌘KCtrlK
