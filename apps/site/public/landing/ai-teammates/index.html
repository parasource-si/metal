ai teammates for a small team with work to finish

hand off the prep and arrive with something ready to decide. give a Metal bot a narrow job and a person who accepts the result.

by the Metal team · checked 12 november 2026 · how we made this

bots keep separate names, chats, memory and ai choices. bring them into a group when their jobs need each other. choose which apps each can reach and what it may remember. work use needs a work license.

fill a gap, not a roster

start where work keeps falling between people. a solo founder may need a second reader more than another writer. a two-person studio may need a clearer brief rather than a bot called 'head of operations'.

try a preparer that turns attached material into a brief. add a reviewer that checks it against the source only when that helps. these are jobs you define, not a pack you have to adopt.

use new bot to give each a name and one job in plain words. each keeps its own chats and memory. edit the job later if it changes.1 the founder guide starts with work you own alone.

put a person at both ends

keep the brief current and the client gets the right work. the bot won't notice the client brief changed on tuesday. a person has to. choose who gives the brief and who accepts the result. that can be the same person.

for a design studio, attach approved client notes and the current draft. ask the preparer for requirements. ask the reviewer to compare the draft with that list. the accepting person reads the notes before contacting the client.

make the handoff rule concrete: 'show the source file, requirement and question that needs a person. never turn a suggestion into an approved requirement.' the accepting person resolves the questions against the source.

use a group when the jobs depend on each other

a room should make the handoff shorter. group chats let bots pass work through @mentions. choose only when mentioned to direct the order yourself. choose leader when a lead bot should assign work.2

for release notes, try these group instructions: 'use the change notes attached to this job. the preparer drafts. the checker flags unsupported claims. bring disagreements to me. ask before changing files or apps. no publishing.'

pin the agreed brief as the group's one pinned item. keep the room small. the group guide explains the answer settings. more participants won't rescue an unclear brief.

a shared room doesn't mean shared access

each bot keeps its own ai and permissions. a reviewer may only need attached files. a preparer might need one connected app. make that choice separately for each bot.

  • apps can be off, read only, or read and write
  • an exact action list narrows access further
  • dusk blocks forbidden changes before they reach the app3

sends ask first unless you set a daily amount. careful with Full access: a bot there with no sending rule can send up to 25 a day without asking. give it Ask every time.8

for app edits, keep read only access or add 'ask me before changing anything'. those changes don't automatically ask.3 group learning stays off until you enable it for that bot. agree what should carry forward, then review notes in memory when jobs change.4

work use has a license and an ai bill

budget for the work license and the ai your team will use. dusk is source-available under polyform noncommercial. personal use on your own computer is free; work needs a work license.56

as of 12 november 2026:

  • cloud pro costs $50 monthly or $500 yearly, including one person's work license
  • team plans start at $100 monthly, with a dedicated machine and shared bots
  • write to hello@dusk.bot about team plans and who needs access7

your provider bills ai usage separately. ongoing work spends that allowance too. compare the buying options with both costs in mind. ask about seats, roles, approvals and activity history before buying if your team needs them.

keep it only if the handoff gets easier

test one real job before adding more bots. give it the material you'd give a colleague. define done. have the accepting person review the result.

note what needed correction: missing context, an unclear brief or an unsupported claim. fix the job when that caused the problem.

if you spend the day untangling replies, shrink the room or split the chats. if jobs overlap, keep the clearer one. client commitments, sensitive people decisions and sign-off stay with a person.

choosing one repeat job instead? start with the small-business guide.

try it in 2 minutes

  1. give a bot a preparation job
  2. attach a short brief and draft
  3. ask it to list requirements and open questions
  4. read the result before adding a reviewer

if you can't say what a reviewer adds in a sentence, improve the first job before adding a role.

mistakes people make

giving every bot the same broad job

name a distinct contribution. remove roles that only repeat another reply.

assuming a group shares app permissions

set access per bot. a reviewer often needs only the attached files.

treating agreement in the group as sign-off

have the accepting person read the source before anything reaches the client.

before you add another teammate

  • the job fills a real gap
  • a person owns the brief and acceptance
  • the bot reaches only the apps it needs
  • group learning is a deliberate choice
  • work licenses and ai costs fit the team

questions

how many ai teammates should a solo founder start with?

start with a useful job. add a reviewer when it contributes something different. keep a group to a few focused bots. give each a distinct task and the context to do it.

can a small team share bots?

yes, on team plans. they start at $100 monthly with shared bots and a dedicated machine. ask hello@dusk.bot about the arrangement, seats and roles you need.

do i need a work license if i run it on my own laptop?

yes, work use needs one. the free local app covers personal use. cloud pro includes a work license for one person. team plans cover shared-bot setups.

can the bots talk to each other without me copying every reply?

yes, through group @mentions. choose leader for a lead bot to assign tasks. use only when mentioned to direct them yourself. each bot keeps its access limits.
