ai email triage: let a bot sort your inbox and draft the replies

hand one bot your inbox and one job: put what needs you on top, file the rest, draft the replies. you send the routine once and it runs every morning.

by the Metal team · checked 12 november 2026 · how we made this

it never sends anything unless you tap allow, so the worst case is a draft you delete. most people get their mornings back within a week.

the routine

this is the only message you need. works with gmail or outlook; connect one first.

paste this into a chat with your bot

you're my inbox helper. every weekday at 7:30, read everything that came into my inbox since yesterday at 7:30.

1. put a label "needs me" on anything from a person that asks me something or needs a decision. list those first, one line each: who, what they want, by when.
2. label newsletters "read later" and receipts "receipts". don't list them.
3. for each "needs me" email from a client, write a short reply in my voice and leave it in drafts.
4. never send, archive or delete anything. if you're unsure where something goes, leave it and tell me.
when
weekdays at 7:30, or say "as mail arrives" for a version that runs on new mail
needs
gmail or outlook connected, with read and write access for this one bot

what comes back looks like this (an example, not a real inbox)

4 need you today

1. sam (acme): wants the revised quote by thursday. draft ready.
2. priya: can you do a call at 3 tomorrow? draft says yes, 3 works.
3. bank: card ending 0412 expires next month. no reply needed.
4. jo (landlord): boiler visit, needs a yes for monday morning.

filed: 22 under read later, 9 under receipts.
not sure: 1 email from an address i don't know, left in the inbox.

one bot, one job

an inbox bot that does nothing else gets good fast. it learns your clients, your sign-off, which senders are noise.

tell it once ("dana approves anything over $500", "recruiters go under read later") and it keeps a note you can edit later.

keep it out of everything else. it doesn't need your slack or your files.

the first week

spend five minutes a day on it now and you'll spend one later.

  1. for the first few days add "list what you would label, but don't label anything yet", and keep the bot on Ask for approval.
  2. correct it in the chat: "shorter", "skip newsletters", "the bank goes under receipts".
  3. once the list looks right, drop the dry-run line so it labels on its own. sending stays behind Allow.

morning or as mail arrives

pick a time, or pick a trigger. a scheduled routine runs when you say. an event routine starts on new mail, like "whenever someone at @acme.com writes, draft a reply".

gmail is checked about every 15 minutes, so "as it arrives" means within 15.

on your own computer it only runs while the laptop's awake and Metal is open. want triage done before you wake? put the bot on dusk cloud.

where it gets it wrong

real people sometimes land in read later. usually someone writing from a new address.

the routine tells it to leave anything it's unsure about in the inbox and say so. that catches most of them. and it can't know what it was never told: a client renamed, a project dead. say it once and it updates its notes.

the guard rails

  • it never sends: sending stops at a card with Allow and Deny, unless you set a daily amount. keep this bot off Full access, where a bot with no sending rule can send up to 25 a day without asking
  • it never archives or deletes in this routine, so nothing disappears from your inbox
  • give it read and write access to your mail app only, and to no other app
  • start on Ask for approval and loosen up once a week of runs look right
  • ⌘. stops it mid-run if something looks wrong

if a real person ends up in read later, say "that's a client" in the chat. it won't happen twice.

mistakes people make

one bot for inbox, calendar and files

split them. an inbox bot with one job learns faster.

letting it archive in week one

labels only. archiving hides mistakes from you.

no "unsure" rule

tell it to leave anything unclear in the inbox and flag it.

what it could save you

roughly 1.5 hours a week, if your mornings look like most

20 minutes triaging each morning
- 3 minutes reading the bot's list
= 17 minutes a day
x 5 workdays = 85 minutes a week

a rough estimate. it assumes:

  • your inbox sort takes about 20 minutes now
  • you still read and send replies yourself
  • week one, spent correcting it, isn't counted

questions

does it work with outlook as well as gmail?

yes. connect outlook in Connected apps and send the same routine. labels become outlook folders or categories; tell the bot which you use.

can it sort two inboxes?

yes. connect both accounts with labels like work and personal, and name the one you mean in the routine, or make one routine per inbox.

will it learn my writing style?

it learns from what you tell it and from your corrections, and keeps notes you can see under the bot's Memory. the more specific you are ("no exclamation marks", "sign off with just my first name"), the closer the drafts get.
