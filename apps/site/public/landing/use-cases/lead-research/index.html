a lead research bot that explains why each company fits

get a shortlist you can explain before you write the pitch. give the bot a customer profile, exclusions and permitted company pages.

by the Metal team · checked 12 november 2026 · how we made this

it can research in its own browser and bring back a source for each reason to include a company. keep unsupported budgets, buying intent and private contact details unknown. use the list to choose the next question, with outreach left out.

the routine

get past "looks like a fit" to the passage that supports it. keep the company queue, customer profile and exclusions in a file the bot can read. this job researches companies without collecting personal addresses.1

paste this into a chat with your bot

each tuesday at 2pm, read the current company list in the lead research project folder. open its permitted company websites in your own browser. find independent design studios that publicly offer client workshops. exclude recruitment agencies, directories and firms outside my stated region.

return a shortlist here with:
- company name and website
- the passage supporting fit, source link and date checked
- a reason to include it and a possible mismatch
- the next question to investigate

mark team size, budget and buying intent unknown unless a source directly supports them. remove duplicate companies. skip unchanged entries unless the file asks for a fresh check. list restricted pages you couldn't review. never guess emails, collect private contacts, add crm records or contact anyone.
when
tuesdays at 14:00, reading the current company list in your project folder
needs
a bot with its own browser; access to the company-list file, customer profile and exclusions; no crm connection

what comes back looks like this (an example, not a real inbox)

fictional research example for marta

company: thimblewick studio
- fit: services page lists client workshops for small teams
- source: services page from the company-list file, checked 12 november 2026
- mismatch: brand workshops; your offer concerns product testing
- unknown: budget and buying intent
- next question: does it run product testing workshops too?

excluded: ruskettle talent desk, a recruitment agency
not reviewed: elm design's services page would not open
no private contacts collected. no outreach.

define fit using evidence you can point to

use a fit rule you can point to on the company website. "offers client workshops in this region" gives the bot something visible to find. "likely to buy" invites a guess. exclude directories and other wrong matches before they enter the list.

require the passage supporting each inclusion. a website alone tells you little about budget or planned projects. use market research first if the customer profile itself needs work.

keep weak matches out of someone else's inbox

drop a weak match before it becomes someone else's unwanted email. this routine leaves the shortlist in chat. it adds no crm records and contacts nobody.

choose the next research question from the evidence. email triage handles a different job: replies to conversations you already have.

decide the outreach rules separately

a public address still belongs to a person. before contacting anyone, consider the rules where you and the recipient are: consent, sender details and opting out. respect requests for no contact.

respect source-site terms and personal data rules too. web scraping keeps collection to named fields. a polished opener cannot decide whether contact is welcome. get legal advice for those decisions.

the guard rails

  • require evidence of fit. no source passage, no shortlist entry.
  • check exclusions first. leave existing customers and do-not-contact companies out.
  • leave unknowns unknown. don't infer personal emails or intent to buy.
  • review outreach rules separately. a research entry is not a send decision.

a smaller list with evidence beats a larger list of guesses.

try it in 2 minutes

  1. put three known company websites in a project file
  2. write one visible fit rule and two exclusions
  3. ask for evidence of fit, without outreach
  4. open one cited source and find the passage supporting fit

quick checklist

  • the source supports the stated fit
  • the company clears our exclusions
  • unknown budget and intent remain unknown
  • i can open the cited source
  • research has not become automatic outreach

mistakes people make

asking for companies "likely to buy"

use observable criteria, such as a named service and region.

accepting a source that proves only the company exists

require the passage that supports the fit rule.

rechecking the same list every week

read new or changed queue entries, or ask for a fresh review.

questions

can a non-technical business owner research leads with a bot?

yes. use plain criteria and a permitted source list. begin with a few companies you know. compare the reasons with what makes those companies a good fit.

is this an ai cold email agent?

no. this routine stops at research. outreach needs its own rules, appropriate access and a decision to send.

can it find personal emails and phone numbers?

this routine leaves them out. it researches company fit without collecting private contact details.
