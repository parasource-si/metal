Metal/routines

meeting prep briefs for outside calls, every weekday at 8

stop opening old email threads as the call starts. every weekday at 8, the bot can read today’s outside meetings and recent email with the attendees.

by the Metal team · checked 12 november 2026 · how we made this

it returns the last exchange, open promises and useful questions in chat. roles come from those records, not guessed biographies. missing context should appear as a gap you can fix.

the message

paste this into a chat with your bot

every weekday at 8, prepare a brief here for each outside meeting today. read my work calendar and mailbox. use the internal email domains in your memory notes to tell internal and outside attendees apart. ask if that boundary is unclear. skip cancelled and internal-only events.

read each event and recent email threads with its outside attendees. give me:
- time, purpose and attendees
- each outside attendee’s role, only as stated in the event or email signatures
- the latest exchange and any explicit promise still open
- questions worth asking, separate from facts

include event and message references. say when you find no prior email or cannot confirm a role. never search personal mail, send anything or change an event.
when
weekdays at 8:00; today’s meetings on the work calendar you name
needs
google calendar and gmail, or outlook calendar and mail; read only for the chosen work accounts

what comes back

an example, not a real run

14:30 call with lena at northstar works
purpose: review the draft support agreement, from the event.
who: lena’s latest signature says operations lead. joel is invited; no role found.
last exchange: lena asked about weekend coverage. your reply promised to confirm.
open promise: no coverage answer found in the messages read.
question: which weekend hours matter?
sources: today’s event and the latest northstar works email thread.

make it yours

  • define outside. list your internal email domains and any partner domains you treat as internal.
  • trim the mail window. try the past month if old threads keep crowding out the current subject.
  • keep it on one screen. ask for purpose, last exchange, open promise and one question.
  • exclude private appointments. name calendars or titles to skip before granting wider access.
  • beat your first call. choose an earlier weekday time and read the card’s next runs and time zone.

when it goes wrong

  • same name, wrong person. use the attendee’s exact email address to find old conversations. leave uncertain matches out.
  • the meeting changed after 8. ask for a refresh before joining. this schedule does not automatically follow later calendar edits.
  • no earlier email exists. that is a valid short brief. attach call notes if the conversation happened elsewhere.
  • the laptop slept through 8. a local run under 12 hours late runs once on return, which may miss the call. open run history or use an always-on machine.

bring the last exchange into the next conversation

walk in knowing what you promised last time. this routine reads the event and earlier email, then brings the open promise into chat. keep roles beside the email signature or event that named them; ask about gaps instead of guessing.

google calendar and gmail work together here. outlook can provide both work calendar and mail instead. keep each connection read only.

read the schedule card’s next runs after sending the request. for a whole-day overview, use daily briefing.

try it in 2 minutes

  1. connect work calendar and mail with read only
  2. tell the bot your internal email domains
  3. ask for a brief on one outside meeting today
  4. find its open promise in the cited email

refresh the brief when the calendar changes after the morning run.

what it could save you

roughly 35 minutes a week under these assumptions

10 outside meetings a week
x 5 minutes finding context
= 50 minutes

10 briefs x 1.5 minutes to review
= 15 minutes

50 - 15 = 35 minutes a week

a rough estimate. it assumes:

  • you currently spend five minutes finding context per outside meeting
  • you have ten such meetings a week
  • each brief takes you 90 seconds to review
  • this is a rough estimate, not a measured result; setup and corrections add time

mistakes people make

treating an old signature as a current role

keep the source beside the role and ask when it matters.

reading every mailbox for every attendee

limit the routine to the chosen work accounts.

questions

will it join the call or record the meeting?

no. this weekday 8:00 routine prepares text briefs from today’s events and earlier email. it does not join or record calls.

what happens when my laptop is closed?

the local bot cannot run while that laptop sleeps. it also stops when dusk closes. dusk cloud is an always-on home. read the routine’s time zone too.

can i use outlook without connecting gmail?

yes. name your outlook work calendar and mailbox. the outlook integration lists the available reads.
