ai employees you can give a clear job

give the bot the chore you keep putting off. in dusk, it has a name, job, chats, memory and the ai you choose.

by the Metal team · checked 12 november 2026 · how we made this

ask it to compare a proposal with a brief or repeat a small review. keep the decisions with a person. work licensing, optional hosting and the ai provider’s bill still belong in the budget.

start with the job that keeps coming back

buy relief from a real chore. can you name its input, result and the point where a person takes over? that tells you more than a roster of “employees”.

checking a proposal against a brief has a finish line. running sales bundles relationships, judgment and promises into two words. start with the former.

in dusk, new bot takes a name and a job in plain words. change that job if it grows too broad.1 a friendly shape helps you find the bot; it does not make it responsible for a business decision.

start with one bot, add a second reader when it earns its keep

get the brief reader useful before building a department. ask it to pull requirements from attached files and flag what the brief leaves open. give it a result you can judge against the original.

if review becomes the bottleneck, add a reviewer with the same source files. ask “which requirements did this draft miss?” rather than “does this sound convincing?”

each bot has its own memory and can keep several threads. attach the relevant files to its chat. ai teammates covers shared conversations and handoffs.

make the first request work before it repeats

an unclear brief gets worse on a schedule. try one job first: “read this supplier proposal against the requirements file. list missing requirements and questions. do not contact anyone or change files.”

look for three things:

  • did it find the right material?
  • did it separate facts from guesses?
  • did it stop at the requested result?

correct the job if it missed. more bots will not fix a vague request.

then try: “every thursday at 4pm, review proposals in this project folder. list unresolved questions here. ask me before changing anything.”

the routine returns a schedule card with next runs. watch the early runs and pause it if corrections take over. local routines need your computer awake and dusk open.6

count ai, licensing and hosting separately

a cloud bill buys a computer, not endless ai usage. dusk has no model of its own. your ai provider bills its usage on your plan. check that provider’s current terms for your chosen sign-in.

work use needs a license even on your own computer. pro includes one person’s work license. for a team, plans start at $100 a month with a dedicated machine and shared bots (pricing).

sintra packages role-based helpers. lindy prices its team offer by active users and credits. match the work to the allowance and commitment. a monthly price is not a finished-work budget.

a human still owns the promise

keep customer commitments and difficult decisions with a person. bots can prepare material for hiring, disputes and public claims. the person checking it still owns what happens next.

a neat draft can misunderstand the whole situation. in a trial, ask:

  • how much did i have to correct?
  • what did it miss?
  • was checking it easier than doing it?

we have no measured time-saving or success-rate result to offer. do not count a job as delegated while you redo it quietly.

if you need someone to build relationships and handle unclear situations all day, that is a staffing decision. a list of bot roles does not answer it.

start with one harmless document

get a useful answer from a harmless file first. choose a non-sensitive document. give the bot only the accounts the task needs.

keep app access read only; if you permit writes, say “ask me before you change anything”.7

keep the trial if the result helps. narrow it if corrections eat the benefit. the cost guide helps budget for the job once it earns its place.

try it in 2 minutes

  1. choose one non-sensitive brief and draft
  2. make a bot whose job is comparing them
  3. ask for missing requirements without editing anything
  4. compare a finding with the original brief

write “what done looks like” before you write the bot’s job title.

does this job belong with a bot?

  • the input is somewhere the bot can read
  • you can describe a finished result
  • a person owns the final decision
  • review takes less effort than redoing the job

questions

can ai employees replace my staff?

do not make that your staffing plan. give bots bounded work with a person checking the result. a job title does not give them human accountability or your staff’s context.

can a non-technical business owner set this up?

yes. create a bot with a name and plain-language job. start with an attached document so the first result is small enough to judge.

is anyone running several agents without spending a fortune?

count the full bill before choosing the roster. dusk’s local app is free for personal use. business licensing, ai usage and optional hosting still need a budget. start with one useful job.

does each bot need the same ai?

no. choose a model for each bot and change it later. its chats, files and memory stay. check the provider’s terms and charges for your setup.
